## The Plan

Jannes was coming over from Berlin for a few days in Stockholm, and we had a very clear idea of what a proper summer visit should look like: meet friends by the water, go running, watch football, and maybe stay out a little too long on one of the nights. Stockholm in late June felt like the perfect setting for that — long evenings, lakes everywhere, and the Women's Euro on TV. This is how the week went.

## Friday: Berlin to Stockholm

![image](/blog/stockholm/stockholm0.jpg)

We arrived late on Friday evening after a flight from Berlin. Stockholm welcomed us with rain, which somehow felt on brand. We made it home, dropped our bags, and went to bed far too late for people who were supposed to have a big Saturday ahead of us.

## Saturday: Schafkopf, Trädgården and a swim at sunrise

![image](/blog/stockholm/stockholm1.jpg)

Saturday started much more relaxed. We met friends at a pier on a lake for some pre-drinks in the sun and played a few rounds of Schafkopf, which has quietly become the default card game whenever Jannes and I hang out.

In the evening we went to Trädgården, one of my favourite outdoor clubs in Stockholm. We got there around 6 PM and somehow stayed until 2 AM. The crowd, the music, and the whole atmosphere were exactly what you hope for on a summer night in the city.

After the club closed, we did what seemed like the only reasonable thing and went for a swim in the lake right next to it. The water was cold, the city was quiet, and it felt slightly unreal to be floating there after hours on the dance floor. We finished the night — or morning — with food from Max at sunrise before finally heading home.

## Sunday: A 6k with Mikko and Haaland beats Brazil

![image](/blog/stockholm/stockholm2.jpg)

Sunday started early and sporty. Together with our friend Mikko, we went for a 6k run followed by a short workout and some intervals. It was a great session, but also the kind that makes you question your life choices for the rest of the day.

We were completely exhausted by the evening, but still sat down to watch Norway beat Brazil in the Women's Euro. Erling Haaland's sister Ingrid scored the winner, which felt like the most Norwegian plot twist possible. Naturally, we played more Schafkopf while watching.

## Monday: The library, Kimchi and another painful run

![image](/blog/stockholm/stockholm3.jpg)

Monday was supposed to be a more productive day. We went to the National Library to study, had a really good lunch at Kimchi, and tried to convince ourselves that we were responsible students.

The illusion did not last long. In the evening we went for another run with 8-minute intervals at 4:30 pace, which was just as exhausting as it sounds. We cooled off with a swim in the lake and then watched Spain vs Portugal at home. At that point our legs had more or less given up on us.

## Tuesday: Pilates, pizza and football outside

![image](/blog/stockholm/stockholm4.jpg)

On Tuesday we studied at KTH, spent some time in a café, and met two friends in the evening for our first ever Reformer Pilates session. I had no idea what I was doing, but it was surprisingly fun — especially watching everyone try to look graceful while shaking on the machine.

We ended the day with pizza at an outdoor restaurant and watched the Argentina vs Egypt match outside. A much calmer finish to the week than Saturday, but exactly what we needed.

## So far

It has been a busy few days of running, studying, swimming, watching football, and catching up with friends. Stockholm is very good at making all of those things feel like they belong in the same week. More to come, hopefully with slightly less interval training.
